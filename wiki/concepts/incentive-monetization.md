# Turning Production Incentives into Cash

An incentive becomes useful financing value only when the production can earn it, document it and collect it through an available legal route. [[wiki/concepts/tax-credit-collateral|Tax-Credit Collateral]] explains the lender’s claim; this page explains the cash conversion.

## Five Different Routes

| Route | How Value Reaches the Production | What to Establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refundable credit | The agency pays eligible unused credit under the refund rules | Tax offsets, election, refund fraction, filing requirements and payment schedule |
| Transferable credit | An eligible buyer pays for the certified credit | Permitted transfer, buyer capacity, settlement price, fees and recourse |
| State purchase | The state purchases an eligible credit under a separate mechanism | Eligible holder, certificate age, price, purchase window and annual capacity |
| Rebate or grant | The administering body pays an earned award | Executed award, eligible costs, funding reservation, verification and reimbursement conditions |
| Tax offset or exemption | The production reduces eligible tax or purchase costs | Claimant tax liability or qualifying purchases; savings do not automatically create a separate cash receivable |

Program names can obscure these differences. The [[wiki/entities/new-jersey-film-incentives|New Jersey]] purchase route, [[wiki/entities/louisiana-film-incentives|Louisiana]] buyback and [[wiki/entities/california-film-incentives|California]] refund election have materially different conditions.

## From Budget to Collection

Start with expenditure categories that qualify under the applicable program. Apply the supported rate to each category, limit bonuses to their eligible base, and apply the relevant caps. Then model the chosen realization route, tax offsets, fees, verification costs and timing. Finally, test the net receipt against the loan’s repayment obligations and controlled account arrangements.

An application, reservation, initial approval, audited claim, issued certificate, accepted transfer and received payment are separate milestones. Record the evidence for the actual stage rather than describing all seven as an approved receivable.

## Analysis and Inference

### An Illustrative Cash Bridge

The following assumptions illustrate arithmetic only. They do not describe a state program, a market quote, a MediaHedge advance rate or a funded transaction.

| Step | Assumption or Calculation | Amount |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Total production budget | Hypothetical project | $10,000,000 |
| Qualified spending | Hypothetical eligible subset | $6,000,000 |
| Gross incentive | $6,000,000 × assumed 30% | $1,800,000 |
| Sale proceeds | $1,800,000 × assumed 92% buyer price | $1,656,000 |
| Verification and sale costs | Assumed combined deduction | ($30,000) |
| Net incentive cash before financing costs and taxes | $1,656,000 − $30,000 | $1,626,000 |

The resulting cash equals 16.26% of the total budget before financing costs and taxes, even though the assumed incentive rate is 30%. A lender then separately determines eligibility, advance, reserves and closing deductions. Net incentive cash is not the same as the amount advanced to production.

### Timing Can Change the Result

For illustration, six additional months on an unchanged $1,000,000 principal balance at an assumed 12% annual simple interest adds $60,000. Actual financing may use different accrual, fixed-interest, fee or extension terms. Model those executed terms in [[wiki/concepts/financier-return-economics|Return Economics]]; do not label the incentive percentage an investment return.

## Evidence to Collect

Keep the application and award, applicable rules, qualified-cost model, payroll and vendor support, audit engagement, filing calendar, certificate, buyer or agency payment evidence, and [[wiki/concepts/cash-control-and-waterfalls|Cash-Control Documents]] aligned. Track changes through [[wiki/concepts/monitoring-and-servicing|Monitoring and Servicing]]. A completion guaranty does not by itself cure an ineligible cost, missed filing or incentive shortfall.

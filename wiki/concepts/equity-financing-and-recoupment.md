# Equity Financing and Recoupment

Equity supplies capital in exchange for negotiated economic and governance rights. A project investor backs a particular production; a company investor backs a business and its assets. Identify which entity issues the interest, what it owns and which receipts fund distributions.

## Agree the Economics Before Raising Money

The [[wiki/concepts/full-financing|Finance Plan]] records the contribution. The investment agreement and [[wiki/concepts/cash-control-and-waterfalls|Waterfall]] determine the return. A percentage of the budget does not automatically confer the same percentage of copyright, voting power or profits.

| Term | Decision to Document |
| --- | --- |
| Capital contribution | Amount, funding dates, conditions, escrow and treatment if the financing fails to close |
| Recoupment position | Which expenses, lenders and other investors receive cash first |
| Pari passu | Which investors share a tier proportionately and the contribution base used for that allocation |
| Preferred return | Flat premium or annual accrual, calculation base, compounding, payment order and unpaid balance |
| Profit participation | Defined receipts and deductions, investor/producer split, and talent shares charged to each pool |
| New money | Approval, dilution, seniority and treatment of additional funds needed to finish the film |
| Governance | Budget changes, additional debt, related-party deals, sales, reporting and enforcement authority |

Keep the return of invested capital separate from the return earned on that capital. A preference determines priority within available cash; it does not create a third-party payment guarantee. [Sundance Catalyst](https://www.sundance.org/initiatives/catalyst/) discusses pari passu investment and documentary backend structures in its own program context.

## Read the Waterfall in Dollars

In the [[wiki/syntheses/film-finance-worked-example|Worked Example]], $587,000 remains after collection fees and senior principal. The contract first returns $400,000 of equity capital, then pays a flat $40,000 preference. The remaining $147,000 splits equally between the investor and producer pools. Investors receive $513,500 in total, or 1.28375 times their contribution. This is a cash multiple, not an annual return.

If only $290,000 reaches the equity tier, investors remain $110,000 short of recovering capital. Neither the preference nor the producer profit tier receives cash. Show this outcome when discussing upside.

## Prepare an Investable Offer

Provide the budget, rights ownership, financing status, distribution assumptions, waterfall, conflicts and downside case together. Select the securities-offering route before soliciting investors. The [SEC’s Offering Pathways](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/offering-pathways) explains registration and exemptions; advertising, investor eligibility and intermediary requirements depend on the selected route. A platform listing is not an exemption.

## Analysis and Inference

Test investor economics after [[wiki/concepts/distribution-economics|Distribution Deductions]], not against public box office. Keep lender repayment, equity distributions, producer fees and tax effects in separate schedules. Require the signed documents and model to implement the same priorities.

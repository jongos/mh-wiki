# Distribution Economics

A film’s public gross and the cash available to its investors measure different things. [[wiki/concepts/pre-sales-collateral|Minimum Guarantees]] can fund production before release; subsequent exploitation generates receipts subject to the distribution contract. Follow each payment through its deductions and rights territory.

## Follow the Revenue Chain

1. Audiences pay exhibitors or platforms, or a buyer pays a license fee.
2. The exhibitor/platform retains its contractual share and applicable taxes or charges.
3. The distributor receives its share or license receipts and applies the agreed fee, recoverable expenses and advance recoupment.
4. A sales agent applies any separately agreed commission and expenses to the receipts it handles.
5. Net producer receipts enter the agreed [[wiki/concepts/cash-control-and-waterfalls|Collection Account and Waterfall]].

The contract determines the order and calculation bases. Do not apply every possible deduction to every revenue stream. An all-rights distributor, a sales agent and an aggregator perform different functions. [WIPO’s film-finance guide](https://www.wipo.int/publications/en/details.jsp?id=4611&plang=EN), chapter 2, explains how distribution rights and agreements connect to financing.

This sequence describes the economic deductions, not a mandatory payment route. A collection account can receive gross receipts and pay authorized fees and expenses directly. Reconcile amounts already withheld by distributors or agents before applying the collection-account waterfall so the same charge appears only once.

## Read the Commercial Terms

| Term | What to Establish |
| --- | --- |
| Rights grant | Media, territory, language, exclusivity, term, release obligations and reserved rights |
| Windows and holdbacks | Which releases can happen when, and whether one license restricts another |
| Distribution fee | Percentage, gross/net base, related-party transactions and subdistributor treatment |
| P&A and expenses | Marketing and release budget, approval threshold, cap, overhead, interest and supporting invoices |
| Advance recoupment | Which receipts recover the MG and whether unused balances carry forward |
| Cross-collateralization | Whether one territory, medium or title pays another’s unrecouped costs |
| Statements and audit | Reporting periods, payment dates, records access, objection deadlines and recovery of audit costs |
| Delivery and termination | Acceptance requirements, cure periods, rights reversion and treatment of unrecouped sums |

## A Simple Net-Receipts Bridge

Assume $800,000 reaches a distributor from newly sold rights after buyer/platform deductions. A negotiated 25% fee takes $200,000; $100,000 of approved expenses leaves $500,000 for the producer’s collection account. These are hypothetical terms, not market averages. The [[wiki/syntheses/film-finance-worked-example|Worked Example]] combines that cash with separate presale and incentive receipts.

Track an MG once as a production financing source or loan repayment asset. The distributor’s recovery of its advance from exploitation receipts is a separate ledger. Do not count the same MG again as incremental revenue, or assume producer overages arrive before the distributor has recouped under the contract.

## Analysis and Inference

Build forecasts by available right and payment counterparty. Reconcile territory schedules to [[wiki/concepts/chain-of-title-and-rights|Rights Ownership]], and keep [[wiki/concepts/ip-monetization-beyond-screen|Merchandising and Interactive Rights]] separate when reserved. Test lower receipts, higher expenses and delayed statements before estimating equity distributions.

# Federal Film and Television Deductions

Section 168(k) can accelerate the deduction of a qualified production’s costs. The benefit depends on the taxpayer’s ability to use the deduction; it provides neither a budget reimbursement nor a transferable incentive certificate. The [[wiki/concepts/proposed-federal-film-tv-credit|Proposed Federal Film and Television Credit]] is a separate legislative proposal.

Official-source review: November 15, 2026; Code through Public Law 119-121 and IRS Notice 2026-11. Apply the law and elections for the taxpayer’s filing year.

## What the Enacted Rule Does

Section 168(k)(1) provides an additional first-year depreciation deduction of 100% of qualifying adjusted basis and reduces basis by the deduction. Public Law 119-21 restored 100% treatment for eligible property acquired after January 19, 2025, subject to applicable transition and eligibility rules. IRS Notice 2026-11 provides interim guidance on that treatment. [Code §168(k)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s168/); [Notice 2026-11, sections 2–4](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-06_IRB).

Section 168(k)(2)(A)(i)(IV) includes a qualified film or television production by reference to §181(d), disregarding §181(a)(2)’s dollar cap and §181(h)’s termination date for this eligibility test. The expiration of the separate §181 election therefore does not, by itself, terminate §168(k) treatment. The $15 million/$20 million §181 limits do not apply to this §168(k) eligibility test.

## Qualified Productions and Ownership

The incorporated §181(d) definition requires 75% of total production compensation to be qualified compensation for services performed in the United States by actors, production personnel, directors and producers. Participations and residuals are excluded from compensation for this test. This is a compensation test, not a test of 75% of the budget, filming days, citizenship or financing sources. [Code §181(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s181/).

For television series, §181(d)(2)(B) treats each episode as a separate production and limits the definition to the first 44 episodes. Productions requiring performer records under 18 U.S.C. §2257 are excluded. Those rules differ from the proposed credit’s season-level definition and photography-day test.

Establish the taxpayer’s ownership, depreciable basis and eligibility under the original-use/acquisition rules. A cash investment or loan does not automatically give the investor or lender deductible production basis. Review entity allocations, basis, at-risk/passive-activity rules, loss utilization and recapture for the specific taxpayer.

## Timing and Transition

| Event or Issue | Treatment to Verify |
| --- | --- |
| Acquisition date for a qualified film/TV production | Notice 2026-11, section 2.03(2)(e), describes the principal-photography commencement rule; section 3 applies the revised January 2025 dates through the referenced regulations. |
| Placed in service | Section 168(k)(2)(H)(i) uses initial release or broadcast, not merely finishing photography or post-production. |
| Photography begins after January 19, 2025 | Potential 100% treatment if all requirements are met. A 2026 release date alone does not establish eligibility. |
| Earlier acquisition; later release | Review the prior-law phase-down and transition rules. New expenditure after the cutoff does not automatically reset the acquisition date. |
| Election out | Section 168(k)(7) permits an election out for the applicable class; filing and classification require review. |
| Transitional reduced percentage | Section 168(k)(10) and Notice section 4.03 address an election for 40% (60% for specified longer-production-period property/aircraft) in the first taxable year ending after January 19, 2025. The 60% category is not a blanket rule for film production. |

Notice section 6 permits reliance for eligible property placed in service in taxable years beginning before the forthcoming proposed regulations are published. Starting with the first year of reliance, follow sections 3–5 in their entirety for all eligible property placed in service in those years. Check later guidance and applicable elections before filing.

## How Section 181 Differs

Section 181 is an elective production-cost expensing provision. Its text contains a $15 million film/TV cost limit, increased to $20 million for specified areas, and an election deadline tied to the return for the year costs are first incurred. Section 181(h) excludes productions commencing after December 31, 2025. For productions that began earlier, review eligibility separately: the commencement cutoff does not prohibit every deduction claimed after 2025.

Section 181(b) prevents a second depreciation/amortization deduction on basis covered by that election. Maintain a cost/basis schedule so §181, §168(k) and other recovery methods do not deduct the same basis twice. [Code §181(a)–(d), (h)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s181/).

## Deduction Is Not Cash Proceeds

**Illustrative assumptions:** a taxpayer has $1,000,000 of remaining eligible basis, qualifies for a 100% deduction, can use it immediately and has an assumed 21% marginal tax rate. The deduction is $1,000,000; the simplified tax reduction is $210,000. Neither amount establishes a refund or cash available to the production before release. Loss limitations, tax attributes, ownership and later basis effects can change the result.

Model the deduction in the taxpayer’s tax forecast. Model collectible incentives and contracted payments separately, with evidence of their amounts and timing. See [[wiki/concepts/tax-credit-collateral|Tax-Credit Collateral]] and [[wiki/concepts/incentive-monetization|Incentive Monetization]].

## From Production Basis to Usable Tax Benefit

Section 168(k) is a tax deduction mechanism, not an application-based film grant. The taxpayer establishes eligibility and reports the deduction through its tax return. The production’s financing plan separately records any cash that taxpayer commits to invest.

| Step | Record to Reconcile |
| --- | --- |
| Identify the tax owner | Entity ownership, production agreements and entitlement to the depreciable asset |
| Build adjusted basis | Capitalized production costs, prior deductions, reimbursements and applicable incentive adjustments |
| Test production eligibility | Domestic compensation calculation, episode count and excluded-production rules |
| Establish the deduction year | Principal-photography commencement, acquisition rules and initial release/broadcast evidence |
| Apply elections | Applicable percentage, election out and any transition election for the taxpayer’s year |
| Test actual utilization | Entity allocations, owner basis, at-risk amounts, passive losses and other applicable loss limits |
| Track remaining basis | Deduction taken, later income, disposition proceeds and recapture analysis |

The [IRS Passive Activity and At-Risk guide](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925) explains the ordering of relevant loss limitations: basis limitations, at-risk limits and passive-activity limits can restrict current use, with further excess-business-loss rules for affected noncorporate taxpayers. An investor’s tax rate alone does not establish an immediately usable benefit. Do not assume that a passive film loss offsets salary income.

## Compare Three Taxpayer Outcomes

For the same hypothetical $1 million eligible deduction, immediate use at an assumed 21% marginal rate produces a simplified $210,000 current tax reduction; use at an assumed 37% rate produces $370,000. If the deduction creates a loss that the taxpayer cannot currently use, current cash tax reduction can be zero. Future use depends on the applicable carryforward and limitation rules. These calculations do not change the $1 million deduction or create a transferable credit.

Accelerated cost recovery also reduces remaining tax basis. Later taxable receipts and a sale of the asset must be modeled separately. [IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946), chapter 3, explains that gain on disposition can trigger ordinary-income recapture of the special depreciation allowance. Do not present the initial deduction as permanent tax-free income.

## Keep the Federal Mechanisms Separate

| Mechanism | Benefit | Production Timing | Financing Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Section 168(k) | Deduction of eligible adjusted basis | Initial release or broadcast, with acquisition and eligibility rules | Taxpayer benefit; count only a separately committed cash contribution |
| Section 181 | Separate elective expensing provision with its own limits | Apply its commencement cutoff and election rules | Maintain a cost schedule preventing duplicate deductions |
| Proposed Section 45BB | Proposed credit on qualified compensation | Introduced text uses completion and a future commencement condition | Zero committed credit proceeds under the current proposal |

The [[wiki/concepts/proposed-federal-film-tv-credit|Federal Credit Proposal]] uses a different domestic-activity test and includes a basis-reduction rule.

## Analysis and Inference

For [[wiki/concepts/full-financing|Full Financing]], count enforceable cash contributions committed to the production, not the face amount of a deduction. Identify the taxpayer receiving the benefit, when it can be used and whether any resulting cash contribution is committed. Verify assignability before treating a tax benefit as collateral.

State incentives and federal deductions have different qualification tests and timing. State conformity, reimbursement/basis effects and anti-double-benefit rules require separate review. A proposed federal credit is not current collateral; its introduced text also contains a basis reduction, so a future combined model cannot simply add a credit to an unchanged deduction.

## Evidence Needed Before Transaction Use

Document tax ownership, episode count, eligible-cost/basis rollforward, domestic compensation test, principal-photography start, initial release/broadcast, prior elections, other incentives and expected deduction utilization. Have tax counsel/accountants reconcile those facts to current authority and the actual taxpayer’s return position.

# Full Financing

A production is fully financed when verified and properly timed financing sources plus committed equity cover the complete production and delivery budget, financing costs, required reserves and credible contingency. Both amount and timing matter: funds must be available before payroll, vendors and delivery costs become due.

## Why It Is the First Credit Protection

Completion and delivery are common dependencies across several repayment paths. [[wiki/concepts/pre-sales-collateral|Pre-sale minimum guarantees]] often require conforming delivery; [[wiki/concepts/tax-credit-collateral|tax incentives]] require qualifying spend and filings; [[wiki/concepts/gap-collateral|unsold-rights value]] depends on eventual licensing and collection; and [[wiki/concepts/completion-protection|completion guarantors]] commonly require an approved, fully financed bonded budget. A gap in production liquidity can therefore impair multiple assets at once.

## Five-Part Test

1. **Complete uses:** include production, post-production, delivery, bond and insurance premiums, interest, fees, reserves and contingency.
2. **Verified sources:** distinguish funded cash, binding commitments, eligible debt and committed equity from deferrals, future fundraising and speculative sales.
3. **Timing match:** prove that each source arrives before the related use through a weekly or monthly cash-flow schedule.
4. **Contingency and overage plan:** identify the cushion, guarantor oversight and party responsible for variance beyond contingency.
5. **Controlled disbursement:** connect draws to approved uses, cost reports, remaining cost to complete and sufficiency of undisbursed funds.

## Read a Finance Plan

The budget records what the production will cost. The finance plan identifies who supplies the money; the cash-flow schedule shows when it can be spent. Keep all three reconciled to the closing statement. [Cinelaunch’s finance-plan guide](https://www.cinelaunch.io/post/crafting-a-winning-film-finance-plan-a-comprehensive-guide) offers a useful presentation checklist, but its sample advances are not underwriting rules.

For each source, record its legal provider, funding type, currency, gross commitment, deductions, net production cash, availability date, evidence and remaining conditions. Keep collateral face value and the approved advance calculation in a linked schedule. Label status explicitly: proposed, conditionally committed, drawable or funded. A press announcement or indicative offer does not establish available cash.

### Cash and Noncash Contributions

[Filmarket Hub’s introductory guide](https://medium.com/filmarket-hub-academy/the-finance-plan-of-a-film-a-breakdown-c5556c176a24) broadens the funding discussion to deferrals, in-kind services and coproduction. Classify each contribution by what it actually provides:

| Contribution | Treatment in the Plan | Evidence to Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash equity, grant or loan | Identify amount, restrictions, conditions and payment date | Executed documents, approvals and funding evidence |
| Deferred fee | A documented change to when a specific cost is paid; no new cash receipt | Amount, consent, due date, priority and any continuing claim |
| In-kind service or equipment | Covers an identified budget item; cannot fund unrelated payroll | Scope, supported value, availability and replacement cost |
| Coproducer contribution | Separate cash, services and any proposed program benefit | Contribution agreement, delivery duties and separate eligibility evidence |

Do not both reduce a cash use and add the same deferral or service as cash financing. Coproduction alone proves neither a funding award nor tax eligibility. A loan closing a budget shortfall is not necessarily a gap loan: the repayment asset determines whether it is presale-backed, incentive-backed or supported by unsold rights.

## Worked Finance Plan

**Worked example: hypothetical amounts in USD.** Assume production, post-production, delivery, insurance and completion costs total $900,000, with $100,000 of contingency. Financing fees and restricted reserves, including the assumed interest provision, add $100,000. Total uses are therefore $1,100,000.

### Reconcile Gross Sources and Uses

Assume executed equity and two approved loan components, subject to the draw conditions below. The collateral schedule supports the stated commitments.

| Funding Source | Gross Commitment | Fees and Restricted Reserves Withheld | Net Production Cash |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cash equity | $400,000 | $0 | $400,000 |
| Presale-backed loan component | $450,000 | $60,000 | $390,000 |
| Incentive-backed loan component | $250,000 | $40,000 | $210,000 |
| **Total** | **$1,100,000** | **$100,000** | **$1,000,000** |

The two reconciliations describe the same plan:

- **Gross basis:** $1,100,000 of sources covers $900,000 of production and delivery costs, $100,000 of contingency and $100,000 of financing fees and restricted reserves.
- **Net basis:** $1,000,000 available to production covers the $900,000 cost budget plus $100,000 contingency. Do not subtract the withheld $100,000 again.

Restricted reserves are funded uses, not freely spendable production cash; release depends on the documents. Underlying presale and incentive receipts repay the loans and are not a second production funding source. Any residual must be separately evidenced, timed and reconciled to the waterfall.

### Verify Availability Before Spending

| Source | Assumed Availability | Required Evidence and Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equity | $400,000 before initial production spending | Cleared contribution and agreed equity-first order |
| Presale-backed component | $390,000 net before principal photography | Executed facility, eligible assigned receivable and satisfied draw conditions |
| Incentive-backed component | $210,000 net before post-production | Executed facility, supported incentive collateral and satisfied draw conditions; this is a loan draw, not the incentive payment |

The simplified schedule below starts with no cash. It shows period receipts before period spending and conservatively includes the entire contingency in the final period’s uses. Actual review needs weekly dates and intraperiod cash lows.

| Period | Opening Cash | Net Funding Received | Production Uses Including Contingency | Closing Cash |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Preparation | $0 | $400,000 | $250,000 | $150,000 |
| Principal photography | $150,000 | $390,000 | $450,000 | $90,000 |
| Post-production and delivery | $90,000 | $210,000 | $300,000 | $0 |

### Analysis and Inference

If the last $210,000 draw arrives after the final period’s uses, the project has a $210,000 cash shortfall despite balanced total commitments. Identify committed replacement liquidity or an approved change to payment timing before proceeding. A larger sales estimate or an unfunded equity promise does not close that cash shortfall.

## Closing and Draw Gates

- Evidence of every credited financing source and its conditions.
- Equity funded in the required order.
- Approved budget, cash flow, delivery specification and closing statement reconciled.
- Required reserves funded and [[wiki/concepts/completion-protection|completion coverage]] effective on the same budget.
- No hidden circularity or double counting among sources.
- Each draw leaves enough committed liquidity to finish and deliver under the current forecast.

## Failure Modes

A sources-and-uses table can balance while remaining unfinanceable. Common failures include conditional or delayed sources, unfunded equity, circular funding, omitted delivery or financing costs, early use of contingency, new deferrals and a cash trough that occurs before a receivable can be bridged or collected. A completion bond does not repair a package that fails the bond's financing conditions.

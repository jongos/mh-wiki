# Why a Production Must Be Fully Financed

## Scope

Financier brief explaining why complete and timely sources-and-uses coverage is a credit prerequisite rather than a production-management detail.

## Key claims

- A production loan is protected only if the project can reach contractual delivery.
- Verified sources and committed equity must cover production, post, delivery, financing costs, reserves and contingency.
- An unfunded gap can impair distributor receivables, tax incentives, unsold-rights value and completion protection at the same time.
- Total balance is insufficient when sources arrive after the production's cash trough.
- Draw conditions must preserve enough remaining committed liquidity to complete and deliver.

## Controls and decision points

- approved complete budget and delivery schedule;
- executed source commitments and funded equity;
- week-by-week or month-by-month cash-flow schedule;
- contingency, overage responsibility and guarantor alignment;
- equity-first, reserve and closing conditions;
- draw tests for default, budget compliance, remaining cost and undisbursed sufficiency;
- consent for material reallocation, new deferrals, withdrawals or reserve use.

## Limits

A balanced table may use conditional, delayed, circular or duplicated sources. Delivery, financing and reserve costs may be omitted. Equity may be documented but unfunded. Deferrals can become blocking claims. Contingency can disappear early. A completion bond does not cure a financing package that fails its conditions.

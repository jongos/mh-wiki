# Pre-Sales as Collateral: A Financier's Crash Course

## Scope

Two-page internal financier brief explaining how an executed pre-sale or minimum-guarantee agreement can become collateral after contract, delivery, obligor, assignment and collection analysis.

## Key Claims

- A pre-sale exchanges defined distribution or license rights for a fixed minimum guarantee, commonly payable after conforming delivery and acceptance.
- The financeable asset is the enforceable, assignable and collectible net receivable, not the headline contract value or a sales estimate.
- Gross amounts are reduced for conditions, taxes, commissions, deductions, setoff, currency, timing, collection expense and unsupported components.
- Direct assignment, distributor acknowledgment, duplicate notices, amendment controls and controlled collections are stronger than a producer's promise to remit proceeds.
- Completion coverage can support the delivery condition, while separate surety or credit protection may address a specifically covered payment default.

## Controls and Decision Points

- confirm the final signed agreement, parties, authority, licensed rights, fixed amount, installments and remaining conditions;
- test delivery, acceptance, rejection, cure, key-element, termination, force-majeure, dispute and governing-law provisions;
- underwrite the distributor's credit and collection history;
- obtain an acknowledged assignment, direct-payment instruction, duplicate notices, amendment controls and lien perfection;
- size only against the verified net collectible amount after timing, currency, tax, deduction and concentration adjustments.

## Limits

Unsigned drafts, offer letters and conditional amounts are not equivalent to executed minimum guarantees. Even an executed agreement may be terminated, disputed, offset, reduced or delayed. The source explains a framework and does not establish enforceability, counterparty credit or eligibility for any specific contract.

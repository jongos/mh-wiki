# Sales Estimates and Gap as Collateral: A Financier's Crash Course

## Scope

Two-page internal financier brief explaining how verified unsold distribution rights and conservatively supported sales estimates may contribute to a gap component before binding licenses exist. It contains undated policy and pricing statements that require current confirmation.

## Key Claims

- A sales estimate is a territory-by-territory forecast, not a receivable, appraisal guarantee or sales-agent payment obligation.
- The collateral is the verified unsold rights and their future proceeds, subject to ownership, availability, delivery, marketability and sales execution.
- The source repeats the stated limits of no more than 50% of supported low value, gap generally no more than 30% of final gross loan, aggregate LTV no more than 60%, gross loan-to-budget no more than 80% and term generally no longer than 15 months.
- As licenses are executed, estimated rights value converts into identifiable contractual receivables.
- The source states that gap inclusion generally contributes approximately three to five percentage points to blended pricing, while actual loan-tape results and transaction mix control observed returns.

## Controls and Decision Points

- verify chain of title, the rights schedule, existing licenses, liens and sales-agent authority;
- test supported low estimates using territory evidence, comparable sales, buyer demand, timing and the agent's actual record;
- remove stale, unavailable, duplicative, promotional, encumbered or unsupported value;
- apply every transaction and portfolio limit and let the tightest result govern;
- secure future licenses and proceeds, control collections and monitor conversion from estimate to receivable.

## Limits

The source does not identify a policy version, approval authority or effective date for its quantitative rails or pricing statement. Values can be correlated across territories and can decline because of market demand, delivery, rights, timing, currency, tax, commission or expense assumptions. Completion coverage does not ordinarily insure a weak sales result.

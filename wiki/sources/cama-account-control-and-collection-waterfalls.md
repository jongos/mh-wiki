# CAMA, Account Control and Collection Waterfalls

## Scope

Financier brief explaining how payment directions, collection account administration, deposit-account control, contractual priority and reconciliation combine to capture film-finance proceeds. It emphasizes that these are complementary rather than interchangeable functions.

## Key claims

- Collateral protects a financier only if proceeds can be identified, captured and prioritized.
- A CAMA allocates receipts through an independent manager; it does not automatically create Article 9 control.
- A control agreement addresses the secured-party relationship with the bank; it does not define the waterfall.
- Payer-level instructions and a source-to-account matrix are necessary because receipts arrive across contracts, territories, currencies and dates.
- Reconciliation must distinguish invoiced, received, cleared, allocated and distributed amounts.

## Controls and decision points

- map every obligor and payment to an approved destination;
- obtain acknowledged directions and restrict unilateral changes;
- model deductions, reserves, senior priority and junior blockers;
- confirm control effectiveness, competing liens and bank setoff;
- reserve consent over accounts, instructions, waterfall amendments, manager changes, deductions and releases;
- match bank cash through the collection manager and loan subledger.

## Limits

Setoff, withholding, manager exculpation, bank liens, chargebacks, FX, sanctions screening, cyber events and broadly defined deductions can interrupt or dilute repayment. Legal documentation must be reconciled to actual bank onboarding and payer behavior.

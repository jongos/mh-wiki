# Where the Financier's Return Comes From

## Scope

Financier brief explaining how coupon, upfront fees, draws, prepayment, extension, nonaccrual, recovery timing and operating friction translate into realized lender performance.

## Key claims

- Stated interest rate is not realized return.
- Short-duration irregular cash flows require date-accurate modeling.
- XIRR, cash multiple, principal-weighted coupon and expected-loss-adjusted return answer different questions.
- Accrued but unpaid interest is not cash yield and may require nonaccrual treatment.
- Gross borrower economics, MediaHedge compensation, financier gross return and financier net return should remain separate.

## Controls and decision points

- build a dated lender-perspective cash ledger;
- standardize balance, day count, fee, nonaccrual, charge-off and recovery definitions;
- weight coupon by funded principal and time;
- model prepayment, extension, default, severity, recovery delay and expense;
- reconcile borrower cash through accounts and waterfalls to the investor ledger;
- attribute results by collateral, obligor, vintage, duration and risk cohort.

## Limits

Simple rate averages are misleading. Upfront fees may overstate annualized economics on short loans. Extensions can raise nominal interest while lowering XIRR. Capitalized and default interest can remain uncollectible. MOIC ignores time, while IRR can overemphasize early small receipts. Gross yield excludes servicing, legal, funding and idle-capital drag.

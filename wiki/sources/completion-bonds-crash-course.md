# Completion Bonds: A Financier's Crash Course

## Scope

Two-page internal financier brief explaining how completion protection can address the risk that an approved production is not completed and delivered. It distinguishes completion risk from casualty, obligor, tax-credit and commercial-performance risk.

## Key Claims

- A completion guaranty ordinarily promises completion and delivery under approved terms or the payment remedy stated in the executed documents.
- The guarantor reviews the script, budget, schedule, team, contingency, production plan and insurance package before issuing coverage.
- Monitoring, corrective action, additional completion funding, personnel replacement and takeover can be available remedies, depending on the documents.
- MediaHedge and its financing partner should be expressly named in the operative guaranty and related endorsements if they are intended beneficiaries.
- Completion protection does not ordinarily guarantee distributor payment, tax-credit realization, audience demand or producer repayment.

## Controls and Decision Points

- reconcile the bonded budget, schedule and delivery specification to the approved financing package;
- confirm the actual guarantor, authority, final executed form, named beneficiaries, covered amount and liability cap;
- align notice, cure, reporting, materials access, takeover, account-control and payment provisions;
- review premium payment, exclusions, claims deadlines, subrogation, lien priority and remedy allocation;
- keep the production fully financed through the current cost-to-complete forecast.

## Limits

Coverage is transaction-specific and can depend on timely notice, premium payment, full funding of the agreed production amount, compliance with the approved plan and claims procedures. The source lists possible exclusions but does not establish that any particular exclusion or remedy applies to a specific transaction. Final executed documents control.

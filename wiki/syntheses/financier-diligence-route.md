# Financier Diligence Guide

## The investment question

For a financier, the useful question is not simply whether a film or television project looks attractive. It is whether the proposed exposure can survive the linked risks of funding, completion, delivery, collateral realization, cash capture, servicing and enforcement—and whether the resulting portfolio economics compensate for the time, loss and operating burden.

This guide turns the wiki into a diligence sequence. It tells you where to start, what each page should help you identify and where the current source record stops.

## Eight diligence gates

### 1. Can the production reach delivery?

Start with [[wiki/concepts/full-financing|Full Financing]]. Identify whether complete and properly timed sources cover production, post, delivery, financing costs, reserves and contingency. Look for the cash trough, equity funding order, overage responsibility and the draw test that preserves enough remaining liquidity to finish.

**Financier output:** a supported financeability finding and a list of unresolved funding or timing conditions.

### 2. What value is expected to repay the exposure?

Use the [[wiki/syntheses/repayment-and-risk-map|Repayment and Risk Map]] to separate contracted receivables, [[wiki/concepts/tax-credit-collateral|tax incentives]], unsold-rights value, insurance proceeds, completion protection and controlled cash. Identify each value source's owner, obligor, conditions, deductions, timing and shared dependencies.

**Financier output:** a repayment schedule that avoids double counting and distinguishes collateral value from risk-transfer protection.

### 3. What is the maximum defensible commitment?

Follow [[wiki/concepts/loan-sizing|Loan Sizing]] from eligible net collateral through asset advances, concentration, aggregate leverage, budget exposure, term and liquidity. The controlling commitment is the lowest amount allowed by the applicable tests—not the amount justified by price or headline value.

**Financier output:** a reproducible sizing table showing the binding constraint, remaining cushion and every requested exception.

> [!caution] Policy freshness
> The quantitative rails in the ingested briefs have no stated effective date or version. Treat [[wiki/syntheses/policy-rails-and-control-matrix|the control matrix]] as a map of source-stated internal policy pending current-authority confirmation.

### 4. Can the financier reach the assets and the cash?

Read [[wiki/concepts/security-package|Security Package]] with [[wiki/concepts/cash-control-and-waterfalls|Cash Control and Waterfalls]]. Match each asset to its owner, jurisdiction, grant, perfection or control method, payer instruction, collection account, waterfall position and reconciliation evidence.

**Financier output:** an asset-to-rights-to-account map and a closing-condition list for every gap.

### 5. Which layer answers each failure mode?

Use [[wiki/concepts/protection-stack|Protection Stack]] to map verification, structural cushion, security, controlled cash, completion support, [[wiki/concepts/production-insurance|Production Insurance]], monitoring and enforcement to distinct risks. Confirm that no instrument is credited for a risk outside its actual terms.

**Financier output:** a failure-mode matrix showing responsive protection, exclusions, shared dependencies and residual exposure.

### 6. How will deterioration become a decision?

Read [[wiki/concepts/monitoring-and-servicing|Monitoring and Servicing]] for the baseline-to-action loop and [[wiki/concepts/forward-flow-governance|Forward-Flow Governance]] for delegated authority and reserved matters. A report becomes a control only when the data is verified, the trigger is defined, the exception has an owner and the response authority is clear.

**Financier output:** a reporting calendar, trigger matrix, exception workflow, consent schedule and continuity plan.

### 7. What happens when the base case breaks?

Use [[wiki/concepts/defaults-workouts-and-recoveries|Defaults, Workouts and Recoveries]] to distinguish liquidity, documentation, project-performance, obligor and collateral failures. Compare cure, protective advance, completion action, extension, sale, claim and enforcement on expected net-present recovery—not nominal claim size alone.

**Financier output:** a rights calendar, remedy comparison, authority record and recovery-accounting framework.

### 8. Does the portfolio diversify and deliver realized return?

Finish with [[wiki/concepts/portfolio-construction|Portfolio Construction]] and [[wiki/concepts/financier-return-economics|Financier Return Economics]]. Look through deal count to common obligors, jurisdictions, guarantors, banks, markets, delivery dependencies and maturity windows. Reconcile coupon and fees to actual dated advances, collections, expenses, nonaccrual, losses and recovery timing.

**Financier output:** concentration headroom, liquidity forecast and return attribution from gross borrower economics to financier net realization.

## Pause points for a financier

The source corpus repeatedly supports pausing when:

- the production balances in total but has an unfunded timing gap;
- collateral has a headline value but eligibility, ownership or collection conditions remain unresolved;
- sizing depends on an undated policy rail, an unclear denominator or circular gap math;
- legal rights and payer routing do not reach the same asset and proceeds path;
- insurance or completion support is credited as if it covered unrelated credit or market risk;
- monitoring produces information without triggers, ownership or decision authority;
- apparent diversification rests on multiple assets sharing the same failure driver;
- expected return is described by coupon while cash timing, losses and operating drag remain unmodeled.

## Analysis and inference

The eight-gate order, suggested outputs and pause-point presentation are a decision-support synthesis. The underlying control statements and limitations come from the cited concept and source-summary pages; the guide does not represent an approval checklist, current policy or a conclusion about any transaction.

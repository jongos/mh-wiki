# Media Finance Lending Landscape

Media finance is an ecosystem, not a single competitor list. Banks and private lenders supply capital, account banks help control cash, production insurers transfer named-peril risk, completion guarantors support production and delivery, and credit or surety providers may protect a specifically defined obligation. The same transaction can use several of these parties, but their promises are not interchangeable.

> [!important] Start with the Role
> A recognized brand does not identify the exact exposure. First determine the product, borrower, repayment asset, legal lender or issuer, lien position, covered obligation and direct claimant rights. Then compare economics and institutional strength.

## A Role-Based Market Map

| Market role | Capital or promise provided | Primary risk addressed | What it does not automatically solve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank or senior lender | Loan, revolver, bridge or asset-backed facility | Timing mismatch and senior credit exposure | Completion, insurer exclusions or commercial performance |
| Specialty project lender | Receivables, incentive, pre-sale, gap, bridge or finishing capital | Project liquidity and collateral monetization | Every collateral class; [[wiki/concepts/gap-collateral\|gap]] remains valuation-dependent |
| Institutional private credit | First-lien, second-lien, mezzanine or structured capital | Larger company, IP, library or contractual-cash-flow exposure | Single-production availability or bank-like treasury infrastructure |
| [[wiki/concepts/production-insurance\|Production insurer]] | Indemnity for covered named perils | Cast, property, equipment, liability, interruption and related events | General overruns, delivery or obligor default |
| [[wiki/concepts/completion-protection\|Completion guarantor]] | Completion-and-delivery guaranty and the remedy stated in the executed form | Failure to complete and deliver within the approved framework | Audience demand or ordinary distributor insolvency |
| [[wiki/concepts/surety-credit-protection\|Credit or surety provider]] | Guaranty of a specifically bonded obligation | Defined payment or performance default | Losses outside the obligation, limit and claim conditions |

## Where Each Role Enters the Transaction

| Stage | Finance question | Relevant parties | Evidence to demand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Development and packaging | Is the project commercially, legally and operationally financeable? | Producer, sales agent, counsel and prospective lender | Chain of title, budget, schedule, estimates, distribution plan and sponsor record |
| Underwriting and closing | What is eligible collateral, who must pay and who bears each covered risk? | Bank or specialty lender, completion guarantor, broker and insurer | Executed contracts, obligor diligence, assignments, policies, guaranties, bonds and interparty terms |
| Production | Does available cash still cover the current cost to complete? | Producer, lender or servicer, production insurer and completion guarantor | Cost reports, hot costs, production reports, claim notices and variance analysis |
| Delivery and acceptance | Have the contractual payment conditions been satisfied? | Producer, distributor, delivery agent, lender and completion guarantor | Delivery schedule, acceptance evidence, cure rights and deemed-acceptance provisions |
| Collection and repayment | Did controlled proceeds reach the agreed waterfall? | Account bank or CAMA, servicer, lender, obligors and any applicable credit provider | Assignment notices, [[wiki/concepts/cash-control-and-waterfalls\|account control]], reconciliations, payment confirmations and claim files |

## Capital Providers

The correct comparison is the product, not the phrase "entertainment finance." Project lending against a receivable is materially different from a corporate revolver, acquisition facility, library loan, mezzanine investment or equity position.

| Banks with production or project capability | [City National Bank](https://www.cnb.com/industries-we-serve/entertainment/film-production.html), [Banc of California](https://bancofcal.com/industries/media-and-entertainment-banking/), [Fifth Third / legacy Comerica](https://www.53.com/content/fifth-third/en/commercial-banking/industries/TMT-solutions.html), [East West Bank](https://www.eastwestbank.com/en/commercial-banking/entertainment), [First Citizens Bank](https://www.firstcitizens.com/commercial/expertise/entertainment-media) and [Western Alliance Bank](https://www.westernalliancebancorporation.com/expertise/entertainment-media) | Production companies, repeat borrowers and documented project or company cash flows | Public service menus do not establish current approval, advance rate, hold size or borrower eligibility |

## Risk Transfer and Delivery Protection

Production insurance and completion support can preserve financeability, but neither supplies ordinary senior loan capital. A market-facing platform may also be different from the legal risk-bearing entity.

| Function | Representative public platforms | Financier's identity check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production insurance | The [Lloyd's market](https://www.lloyds.com/about-lloyds/our-market), [Arch](https://www.archgroup.com/offering/entertainment/production/), [AXA XL](https://axaxl.com/insurance/products/media-and-entertainment-insurance), [Chubb](https://www.chubb.com/us-en/business-insurance/film-producers.html), [Intact Specialty](https://www.intactspecialty.com/intactspecialty/industries/entertainment) and [Allianz Commercial](https://commercial.allianz.com/solutions/entertainment-insurance.html) | Identify the policy issuer, syndicate participation, limits, deductibles, endorsements, claims authority and any material reinsurance dependence |
| Completion guaranty | [UniFi Completion Guarantors](https://www.unifibonds.com/who-we-are/), [Film Finances International](https://www.filmfinances.com/about), [Paterson James](https://www.patersonjames.com/services/completion-guarantee/) and [Intectus](https://www.intectus.de/completion-bond/) | Identify the service platform, legal issuer or carrier, beneficiary, liability cap, remedies, exclusions, claim path and interparty rights |
| Targeted payment protection | Transaction-specific surety, credit or media-obligation product, if available | Identify the principal, surety or insurer, obligee, bonded obligation, trigger, penal sum or policy limit and post-payment rights |

## Finance Committee Comparison Questions

1. **Product fit:** Is the exposure a single production, company revolver, library or IP loan, corporate cash-flow facility, mezzanine investment or equity?
2. **Repayment asset:** Which executed contract, incentive, controlled revenue or enterprise cash flow repays principal, and what is excluded from eligibility?
3. **Capital authority:** Which legal vehicle commits the funds, and what approval, hold-size or syndication dependency remains?
4. **Risk carrier:** Which legal entity signs the policy, guaranty or bond, and is the visible brand an insurer, MGU, broker, risk manager or service company?
5. **Legal priority:** Who holds the lien, copyright mortgage, account control and assigned rights, and how do lender and guarantor rights interact?
6. **Coverage mechanics:** What obligation, limit, exclusion, deductible, notice deadline, cure right and remedy election applies?
7. **Normalized economics:** What do coupon, OID, fees, reserves, capitalized interest, extensions and actual timing produce on an [[wiki/concepts/financier-return-economics|XIRR basis]]?
8. **Conflicts and recovery:** Which party also produces, brokers, advises, services, invests or decides claims, and what happens after delay, abandonment, obligor default or claim dispute?

## Classification Errors to Avoid

- Treating Lloyd's as one insurer rather than a marketplace in which syndicates underwrite risk.
- Treating a completion service company, MGU or risk manager as the legal carrier without reading the guaranty.
- Assuming [[wiki/concepts/production-insurance|production insurance]] guarantees completion.
- Assuming a [[wiki/concepts/completion-protection|completion guaranty]] makes a distributor pay after delivery.
- Counting acquired or combined platforms twice.
- Comparing project debt with equity or company-level private credit without matching borrower, collateral, lien, duration and loss severity.
- Treating a provider website as a current credit box, capacity commitment or transaction approval.

## Evidence Boundaries

This is a capability map as of August 10, 2026, not an endorsement, league table, capacity survey, pricing survey or credit opinion. Provider websites describe their own capabilities. Current ownership, licensing, product availability, capacity, financial strength and underwriting appetite should be refreshed before use. Executed loan documents, policies, guaranties, bonds, endorsements and interparty agreements determine the actual exposure and remedy.

## Analysis and Inference

The groupings above are a reader-oriented synthesis. They identify the most comparable role and product from public descriptions; they do not determine which provider is best for a transaction or establish that any named provider will approve, fund or cover it.

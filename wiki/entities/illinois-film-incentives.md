# Illinois Film and Television Incentives

Illinois requires a cost-by-cost model because resident spending, eligible nonresident compensation and conditional bonuses can earn different rates.

## Program Snapshot

The film office lists 35% on qualified Illinois vendor spending and resident wages, and 30% on limited eligible nonresident wages. Wage eligibility is capped at $500,000 per worker, with additional role and cast-count limits. Separate additions include 15 percentage points on qualifying disadvantaged-area wages and specified 5-point regional, relocating-series and certified-green-production benefits. Each has its own base and conditions. See [Illinois Film Production Tax Credit](https://dceo.illinois.gov/whyillinois/film/filmtaxcredit.html).

## Route to Cash

The credit is transferable under Illinois rules. The transfer regulation permits an applicant’s transfer within one year after the credit is awarded and defines earning by certificate issuance. Confirm the applicable issuance date, transfer approval, fees and buyer tax-use rules. See [Illinois Administrative Code, Section 528.85](https://www.ilga.gov/commission/jcar/admincode/014/014005280000850R.html) and the [film-office FAQ](https://dceo.illinois.gov/whyillinois/film/filmtaxcredit/faqs.html).

## Analysis and Inference

Tag each wage by residency, role, compensation limit and bonus eligibility. Avoid applying a resident-hiring bonus to all purchases or counting the same cost twice. Reconcile the production and loan-out withholding evidence before treating payroll as qualified, and model actual sale deductions rather than treating the credit as a state cash refund.
